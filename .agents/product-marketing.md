## Product Overview

**One-liner:** GoToGuy Solutions is a Canadian digital solutions and marketing service company that gives small businesses one dependable place to go for practical digital help.

**What it does:** GoToGuy helps small and local businesses address website, SEO, local visibility, marketing, content, video, AI, research, data analysis, technology, and digital operations needs. The intended value is not to sell every service to every customer. It is to understand the business problem, handle the appropriate work, or help identify the best path so the owner can stay focused on running the business.

**Product category:** Practical all-in-one digital solutions company for small and local businesses. Depending on the customer's immediate need, relevant search categories may include website services, local SEO, Google Business Profile support, digital marketing services, AI implementation, video production, research and analysis, and business technology support.

**Product type:** Canadian service business with recurring and project-based digital services.

**Business model:**

- A low-cost website subscription is intended to be an accessible entry point.
- Additional recurring or project work is offered when it genuinely benefits the client and falls outside the base website scope.
- The preferred base website model is **$19.99 CAD per month with a 12-month commitment**.
- An upfront signup or setup payment approximately equal to one additional month may be charged for initial setup and domain-related costs. **Needs confirmation:** exact amount, name, tax treatment, payment timing, refund or cancellation terms, and whether it applies in every case.
- The base offer is a defined standard small-business website, not unlimited custom development.
- **Needs confirmation:** the exact page limit, revision allowance, delivery timeline, hosting and maintenance inclusions, content responsibilities, support level, renewal terms, post-term monthly price, cancellation process, and all other contractual parameters.

### Website Subscription Scope

**Included in principle:** Ordinary features that are easy to implement should generally be included rather than separated into small add-on charges. Within a clearly defined standard website scope, these may include:

- Standard business pages
- Contact forms
- Reviews and testimonials supplied and authorized by the client
- Photo and portfolio galleries
- Booking or appointment integration
- Embedding a client-supplied video
- Basic business information and service presentation
- Mobile-responsive design
- Normal navigation
- Standard calls to action
- A blog section when appropriate

**Important scope distinctions:**

- Blog functionality may be part of a website. Writing ongoing blog articles is a separate recurring content service.
- Embedding or placing a client-supplied video may be included. Producing, filming, editing, or creating video is a separate Video Production service.
- **Needs confirmation:** which of the items above are guaranteed inclusions versus case-by-case inclusions, and the limits that define a standard website.

**Paid add-ons or out-of-scope work may include:**

- Pages beyond the defined standard scope
- Unusually large or complex pages
- E-commerce
- Advanced forms or custom functionality
- Ongoing SEO services
- Content writing
- Video production
- Advanced integrations
- AI features
- Specialized automation
- Custom development
- Other work that materially exceeds a standard small-business website

Ordinary, easy-to-build website features should not automatically be treated as paid add-ons.

**Domain policy:** If GoToGuy registers a domain for a website subscription client, GoToGuy may retain control of it during the initial 12-month term. After that commitment, the domain can be transferred to the client. This should be disclosed clearly without dominating the sales message. **Needs confirmation:** domain ownership language, transfer procedure, fees, renewal responsibility, timing, and treatment of early cancellation.

### Service Architecture

The service categories below make the offer understandable and search-friendly. They are not a reason to push every service onto every customer.

1. **Website Services:** Small-business websites, redesigns, landing pages, maintenance, improvements, conversion improvements, website content, troubleshooting, domain and hosting guidance, and WordPress-related services.
2. **SEO and Local Search:** SEO audits, local and on-page SEO, keyword research, purchase-intent and service page optimization, local service pages, technical SEO, schema, directory citations, Google Business Profile optimization, review strategy, AI search optimization, and optimization for discovery in Google AI results and large language models.
3. **Google Business Profile and Local Visibility:** Categories, services, descriptions, service areas, reviews, posts, photos, local optimization, directory consistency, citations, Manta, and other relevant directories.
4. **Marketing:** Strategy, promotional planning, social media content, advertising support, campaign concepts, offers, customer acquisition ideas, local promotions, competitive positioning, and sales messaging.
5. **AI Services:** Practical AI implementation and training, use-case identification, AI-assisted workflows, automation, AI agents, AI employees where appropriate, process improvement, and MCP or connected-tool implementation where useful. AI should be framed as practical business improvement, not hype.
6. **Research and Analysis:** Competitor and market research, SWOT analysis, environmental scans, customer research, performance analysis, trend analysis, opportunity analysis, data analysis, and practical recommendations.
7. **Video Production:** Video editing, long-form edits, short clips, promotional videos, intros, outros, social video, website video production, drone footage integration, and repurposing long videos into shorter content.
8. **Technology and Digital Support:** Troubleshooting, digital tools, business technology guidance, setup help, integrations, software selection, and general digital problem solving.

**SEO strategy preference:** Prioritize purchase-intent and conversion-oriented pages around actions and searches such as buy, book, hire, compare, service near me, local service terms, and city-specific service searches. Blogging can support SEO, but it should not automatically be treated as the primary SEO strategy.

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** Primarily small businesses, local service businesses, and owner-operated organizations without an internal marketing or technology department. Likely segments include trades, health and wellness providers, hospitality businesses, restaurants and accommodations, professional services, local retailers, and companies with outdated or ineffective websites.

**Geographic focus:** Niagara Region, Ontario, including Niagara Falls, Fort Erie, Welland, Port Colborne, St. Catharines, and surrounding Niagara communities. GoToGuy may serve clients outside Niagara when work can be delivered remotely. **Needs confirmation:** priority order among communities, practical service radius, and target markets outside Niagara.

**Decision-makers:** The owner or operator is the likely primary user, champion, decision-maker, and financial buyer. **Needs confirmation:** whether office managers, marketing coordinators, general managers, or other stakeholders commonly evaluate or approve work.

**Primary use case:** A business owner knows something digital needs attention but lacks the time, expertise, or desire to choose tools and coordinate several specialists. They want one dependable provider to understand the problem and move the right work forward.

**Jobs to be done:**

- Help me establish or improve the digital basics that bring in and serve customers.
- Take practical digital work off my plate so I can focus on operating the business.
- Help me understand what I actually need, then handle it or identify the best path without unnecessary complexity.

**Use cases:**

- Launch a defined, standard small-business website with a low initial financial barrier.
- Redesign, repair, maintain, or improve an outdated or underperforming website.
- Improve local visibility through Google Business Profile, directories, reviews, local pages, and related SEO work.
- Create purchase-intent pages that help people buy, book, hire, compare, or find a local service.
- Get practical marketing, content, video, research, data analysis, technology, or AI support without coordinating a separate specialist for every need.
- Investigate an unusual digital or operational problem and receive a practical recommendation or implementation path.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Small-business owners often know their website, Google presence, SEO, marketing, content, technology, or use of AI needs improvement, but they do not have the time or specialist knowledge to diagnose and coordinate everything themselves.

**Why alternatives fall short:** These are positioning hypotheses and should not be presented as universal facts.

- Coordinating multiple specialist vendors adds time, handoffs, and complexity.
- Large-agency proposals may be difficult for some small businesses to justify.
- A single-specialty provider may address one task without connecting it to the rest of the customer's digital presence.
- DIY tools still require the owner to make choices, learn systems, create assets, and complete the work.
- Doing nothing allows outdated or ineffective digital assets to remain unresolved.
- Technical choices can make it hard to tell which services are useful and which are unnecessary.

**What it costs them:** Confirmed quantitative costs are not available. Likely costs include owner time, delayed improvements, fragmented work, unresolved problems, and missed opportunities. These are reasonable problem hypotheses, not measured GoToGuy customer outcomes.

**Emotional tension:** Overwhelm, uncertainty about whom to call, concern about wasting money, frustration with unresolved tasks, and reluctance to commit thousands of dollars before understanding whether an approach will work.

## Differentiation

All differentiators in this section are **intended positioning**, not proven claims.

**Key differentiators:**

- One provider across many everyday digital needs
- A low financial barrier to enter through the website subscription
- Practical support that connects marketing and technology needs
- Flexible additional services when they genuinely benefit the client
- Personalized, local-business-oriented support
- The ability to research and help solve unusual business problems
- A broader range than a single-specialty freelancer, with a more direct and accessible intended experience than a large agency

**How we do it differently:** Start with the problem the owner is trying to solve rather than a predetermined service to sell. GoToGuy can then handle appropriate work or help figure out the best path, while keeping services organized into clear categories.

**Why that is better:** The intended benefit is less time spent identifying, briefing, and coordinating different providers, along with clearer and more practical help. This benefit still needs customer validation and supporting proof.

**Why customers choose us:** **Needs confirmation through sales conversations and customer interviews.** The working hypothesis is that customers will value dependable, plain-English help, a low-barrier website starting point, broad problem-solving capability, and one place to return with digital needs.

## Objections

These are anticipated objections, not objections documented from sales calls. Responses are internal guidance and must be aligned with finalized terms before public use.

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| “How can a website cost only $19.99 per month?” | Explain that it is a clearly defined standard small-business website with a 12-month commitment, not unlimited custom development. State the inclusions, limits, upfront payment, and add-on pricing plainly once those terms are finalized. |
| “Will I own my website and domain?” | Explain the finalized ownership, hosting, data, content, and transfer terms clearly. If GoToGuy registers the domain, disclose that it may remain under GoToGuy's control during the first 12 months and can be transferred afterward. Other ownership details still need confirmation. |
| “Are you going to sell me services I do not need?” | Start with the business problem and recommend only appropriate work. The model supports additional services, but the strategy is not to sell every available service to every customer. |
| “Can one provider really handle all of this?” | Be specific about what GoToGuy will handle, what requires a separate scope, and when the best path may involve another solution. Do not claim experience, capacity, credentials, or results that have not been confirmed. |
| “Why not use a DIY builder, freelancer, or agency?” | Frame the choice around the customer's needs. GoToGuy's intended advantage is one dependable, practical point of contact with broad digital capability, not that every alternative is wrong for every customer. |

**Anti-persona:**

- A customer seeking unlimited custom development within the $19.99 monthly base website price
- A customer whose project requires enterprise-scale procurement, staffing, compliance, or implementation that GoToGuy cannot currently verify it supports
- A customer who expects guaranteed rankings, revenue, leads, or other outcomes
- A customer unwilling to provide required information, approvals, access, or payment
- A customer looking only for the absolute cheapest provider, regardless of fit, trust, or scope
- **Needs confirmation:** minimum fit criteria, prohibited industries, project-size limits, capacity limits, and other disqualifiers

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** An outdated or ineffective website, weak local presence, unresolved technology tasks, lack of time, confusing choices, disappointing vendor experiences, or the burden of coordinating several providers.

**Pull:** One dependable place for practical digital help, a simple website entry offer, plain-English guidance, broad capability, local understanding, and the possibility of an ongoing relationship.

**Habit:** Continuing to do the work personally, relying on familiar tools or informal help, postponing decisions, keeping an existing provider, or accepting the current website and visibility because changing feels like more work.

**Anxiety:** Concern about the unusually low website price, hidden charges, ownership and domain control, service quality, scope limits, disruption, trust, provider capacity, and whether broad capability means insufficient depth. These concerns require clear terms and proof, not unsupported reassurance.
